01 · What this is

A small studio's retelling of the oldest adventure story

Nobodyssey is an 8-bit adventure game made from Homer's Odyssey, chapter by chapter, by one person and the AI working beside him. This page is Book One of four: the first stretch of the voyage home, from the night inside the wooden horse to the morning you sail away from the Cyclops' island.

Private beta means the whole of Book One is here and playable start to finish, by invitation, and we are listening. Keep it to the people we invited. It is free, and will stay free. What you tell us shapes what we fix before Book Two.

The name. Odysseus tells the Cyclops his name is Nobody, and it is the lie that saves him. The game is about the man who had to become nobody to get home.

What it is not. Not a film tie-in, not a copy of any other game. Every sprite, tune and line was made for this, from the Greek text, with the old translations open beside it. There are no purchases, no ads, no accounts, and nothing leaves your device.

Who it is for. Teens and grown-ups who grew up on handheld adventures, or who read the Odyssey once and want to feel it under their thumbs. Rated Teen: fights land, men can die, never in gore.

02 · The story so far

The way home is longer than the war

Ten years at Troy end in the belly of a wooden horse. Thirty men in the dark, one lamp, a city hauling you through its own gate. By dawn Troy is burning and the war is over. Twelve ships, six hundred men, and the sea between you and Ithaca.

Book One follows the first four landfalls, as Homer tells them. At Ismaros the men sack a city and stay too long for the wine, and the Cicones come back at dawn with horns. On the Lotus Shore three scouts eat the flower and forget they ever had a home. In the Cyclops' cave a giant eats your men two at a time, and the only way out is a sharpened stake, a jar of wine, and a name that is not a name.

You play Odysseus. Not a superhero: a captain who counts his men every night, and loses some. The ones you lose stay lost. The game keeps their names in the Roll so you remember them too.

03 · How to play

Five minutes to learn, an hour to finish

  1. Open the game with the Play button. On a phone, turn it sideways; the picture gets bigger and the controls sit under your thumbs. On a desktop, the keyboard works at once.
  2. Press New game. The first minute inside the Horse teaches one move at a time: a small card names the button you need the moment you need it. If you stand still for a few seconds, the right button pulses.
  3. Read the goal line at the top of the screen. It always says the one thing you are doing now. The chart between chapters shows where you are on the voyage.
  4. Lose a man, keep going. A torch is a checkpoint; if something goes wrong you rewind a few seconds, not minutes. Your place is saved on the device at every torch, and Continue on the title picks it up, even after you close the browser.
  5. Listen. A cough in the dark, the giant's snore, the horns up the river: the sounds are tells. Sound on is the way it is meant to be played; Captions and Mono are in Settings if you need them.

The controls

StickLeft thumb. Move, any angle. It floats to wherever your thumb lands.
X · JumpLeap, hop, climb. Hold for a higher leap.
A · HandCatch, hold a man, use, pick up, strike.
B · VoiceA hush in quiet places, a call in loud ones.
Y · PowerAsk the gods at a torch. The bow: hold to draw, aim with the stick, let go.
MenuPause, settings, the Roll, the Voyage Book, credits.
KeyboardArrows move, Space jumps, Z hand, X voice, L power, C menu.
GamepadLeft stick and the four face buttons, the same way.

Settings: stick size, left-handed swap, button opacity, text size, text speed, read aloud, less motion, music and sound levels, challenge (Gentle, Standard, Bold).

05 · Inside Book One

What you are getting

LengthAbout an hour at a player's pace: four chapters, nine side-view levels, the voyage and the hubs between them.
The sourceHomer's Odyssey, Books 9 and 12, from the Greek with Murray and Butler open beside it. Nothing from any film.
Your menSix hundred names on twelve ships. The ones you lose stay lost; the Roll remembers them.
The economyBronze, cups, tripods, cloaks and wine, won in play and spent on offerings and gifts. Nothing is bought with real money, ever.
The relicsEach ship's crew holds a relic for the one who earns their trust. The Swallow's bowstring is the first. Twelve of them make something, in Book Four.
The godsAthena, Poseidon, Apollo and Hermes answer at the torches, each in their own way, and not always kindly.
SoundFourteen songs, the wind and the sea under them, every effect made on your device as you play.
Tech320 × 180 pixels, plain code, one file, no download, works offline. Phone, tablet, desktop, gamepad.
Made byPeople and AI together, under one person's eye, at AZTMM.
