Overview

Emberhold is a medieval story adventure in real 3D, made for the browser on PC and phone. You play Robin: running, climbing, sliding, jumping and sneaking through yards, galleries and fields, and meeting the people of the valley.

His rake and the lid of a coal tub move like a sword and a round shield. Every move is shown first on something harmless by someone with a reason to say it. The fire in his chest drinks the warmth out of a soldier's ember pouch with each answered blow, and when it is let out it throws men back.

It is a story about what you owe the ones who trusted you.

The making

Where the idea came from

Emberhold began on 1 November 2026 with a title and one rule: fights happen on the ground of the world, with no separate battle screen. An early fight played with cards was dropped within the day for plain action in the world.

A week later the story was rewritten from the premise up, to give it more feeling and a reason to care. Two things survived the rewrite: a valley that has never had a winter, and something asleep under the castle. The land was named Hollinshire, from the old words for holly and shire, after the tree that keeps its green through the cold.

It has been built three times: first as a 2D 8-bit game, then hand-drawn, and now in real 3D.

Built with
Plain JavaScript, drawing real 3D in the browser with WebGL through the three.js library, with sound through Web Audio. It ships as a single page. No game engine.
Chapters
One chapter is playable so far, The Warm Court: ten beats in a single castle yard, about ten minutes.

Story

Hollinshire is a valley that has never had a winter. Under the castle at its top, Emberhold, something the size of a hill sleeps. It came down out of the north in a storm a thousand years ago, nearly dead, and the hill people sang it to sleep. Its warmth has kept the whole valley green ever since.

Robin is fifteen. He was found as a baby on the stone of the Warm Court and raised by the castle kitchen. He rakes the ash, and every morning he says good morning to the sleeper through a grating in the yard floor. Lord Ivo, who runs the castle, took a liking to him and taught him to read.

The first chapter, The Warm Court, begins before light. Nell, nine, is asleep against Moss, her dog, on the warm flags. Robin knocks live coals out of the ash, drills with Captain Raine and runs the yard wall with the Lord's Ketil watching. Then the steam from the grating stops, and snow falls in the Court for the first time in a thousand years.

Down in the vault, Lord Ivo has taken men and chain to cut the fire out of the sleeper while it lies still. Robin hammers on the grating and shouts for it to wake. It breathes its fire up the shaft and into his chest, and goes still.

Ivo needs Robin alive and willing, so he keeps Nell close. The gate is barred. Robin goes over the roofs, drops from the wall to the road and walks on with Moss beside him. Behind him, Nell shouts to the dog: "Go with him." Robin says: "We're coming back for her."

World

Hollinshire is a region, not a dale: a green lowland about twenty-five miles from north to south, walled on the north by a snow range, on the west by the scarp of a high moor, on the south by a band of hard hills and on the east by old forest running down to the Grey Sea.

Four rivers come off the high ground and meet. Two fill a great lake, Haverwater, and the others join the Lew at the market town of Brigg. Every road in the game is measured on this map.

The valley's warmth is the sleeper's. Where it stops, at the pass called Rake Nick and the shut gate called the Yat, the real seasons begin. The peoples of the valley live on either side of that line.

Press a map to open it. Drag to move around it; scroll, pinch or use the buttons to zoom.

Cast

  • Robin

    The emberward

    Fifteen, found as a baby on the stone of the Warm Court and raised by the castle kitchen. He rakes the ash and says good morning to the sleeper every day. "Morning."

  • Nell

    Yard child

    Nine, the other foundling in the yard and the nearest thing Robin has to a sister. Bossy and blunt. "Morning, downstairs!"

  • Hugh Raine

    Captain of the levies

    Forty-six, from Milnthorpe. He drilled Robin every dawn for five years and keeps looking at the vault door. "Post. Three."

  • Ketil

    First ward of the Lord

    Thirty-four, made a Hird man at sixteen. He taught a five-year-old Robin to climb the yard wall. "Higher. You will split your knuckles."

  • Lord Ivo Haldane

    Lord of Emberhold

    Raised Robin as his ward and taught him to read. Warm and quiet, he asks questions where others give orders. "Letters tonight. Don't forget."

  • The Hird

    Sworn men of the House

    Quiet, competent soldiers in cold iron, with the hound's head on the shield. They have faces, never hoods. "Easy, lad. Nobody wants you hurt."

  • A collier

    From Wheal Rose

    The colliers built the vents and lamps of the castle, and two of them carry tongs and chain across the Court at the start of the first chapter.

  • Moss

    Nell's dog

    Rough, brown and half-grown. He never speaks and nothing is subtitled. When Nell shouts "Go with him", he goes.

The peoples of Hollinshire

  • The Hird

    Sworn men of the House of Haldane

    They took the dale about the year 200 and sealed the cave about 250. Cold-iron mail that frosts in a warm room, an oxblood surcoat, no hoods.

  • The Levies

    Dalesmen called to the spear

    Tired men far from home, in coats with ember pouches at the belt.

  • Caldwell

    The company of the lit hearth

    The ford-keeping households of the cold keep above the Stanners. Their mark is a robin.

  • Wheal Rose

    The colliers of the Bal Hills

    They came about 450 and built every vent-work and lamp in the valley, and want the old debt paid. A candle in clay on the cap and soot to the elbow.

  • The Outby

    The people of the Moss

    Shut out of the valley in the old hard winters, they learned to live in them. All of them wear the red and grey weave.

  • Moel Gân

    The singers of the hill

    The first people of the valley, who found the sleeper and sang it down. A copper lamp at the belt and fleece braided in the hair.

  • The Dalesfolk

    Brigg, Milnthorpe and the floor

    Let in through the Yat to farm the warm valley floor for rent. Straw hats, bright aprons, wide windows with no shutters.

  • The Household

    The kitchens and yards of Emberhold

    Castle-born servants, and the only people barefoot on stone. Robin is one of them by upbringing.

Places

Emberhold
The castle at the head of the valley, built about the year 250 on a rock knoll over the mouth of the great cave. Its walls are warm to the touch. The Warm Court lies under the great hall, with the grating in its floor.
Milnthorpe
A hamlet of nine houses and a water-mill, seven miles south of the castle on the North Road, in orchards that flower twice a year. The first chapter starts on the road below it.
The Stanners
Where the North Road crosses the river on a causeway of grey stones, two and a half miles below Milnthorpe. The river runs in braids across a mile of gravel and gathers into one stony ford.
Caldwell
A watch-keep on the hill above the ford, inside a timber palisade with two lamps at its gate. The name means the cold spring.
Brigg
The market town where three rivers meet and the North Road crosses on a stone bridge of five arches. Timber and plaster, wide windows, amber lamps in iron cages.
Wheal Rose
The colliers' town on the north face of the Bal Hills, cut into the rock along the warm seams, with rails down every street.
Moel Gân
A lone dome of a hill, 2,880 feet, standing out from the foot of the snow range. The singers chose it because a voice on its top carries down into the cave country.
Haverwater
A lake eleven miles long under the scarp of the Moss, fed by the Skell and the Brune.
The Hoar Fells
The snow range across the north, four to six thousand feet on its crest, with three glaciers and a snowfield.
The Grey Sea
Open sea along the whole east and south of the map, with a firth, islands, skerries and sands.
