Overview

Dyeworks has one hundred levels in five sets of twenty. You tap a vat, then tap another, and all the dye of one colour on top pours across, as much as fits. Dye pours only onto its own colour or into an empty vat. Fill a vat with one colour and it is corked.

The first levels teach by showing. A drawn hand makes the first pour, and a page of four small pictures, How to play, is always one tap away.

New glass arrives every few levels: a small jar, a tinted jar, murky dye, a narrow neck, and long vats of five layers. Every level has three boards, and the game deals a gentler one after a hard patch.

Every fifth level carries a dyer's order: fill it without Undo, cork one colour first, or leave the marked jar empty. Keep the order and a cloth is hung on the washing line behind the vats. Each solve earns up to three drops, there are twenty awards to earn, and a daily vat.

Undo, restart and a hint are always in reach. Leave a board half done and it waits for you: the next time you open the game, Continue puts you back on it, move for move.

The making

Where the idea came from

Dyeworks began on 11 November 2026 as one of a handful of one-level samples, made to find the next small game after Setstones. It was chosen to go on, with one request: that it teach itself. So the first things built for the full game were the lesson, where a drawn hand makes the first pour for you, and the page of four small pictures called How to play.

Games of pouring are usually test tubes in a laboratory. This one is a dyer's yard at dusk: glass vats on a wall, corks, and a washing line that fills with cloths as orders are kept.

Built with
Plain JavaScript, drawn on an HTML canvas, with sound through Web Audio. It ships as one file that opens in any modern browser. No engine and no libraries.

Story

A dyer's yard at the end of the day. The sun has gone behind the roofs, the lamps are lit in the houses across the lane, and the day's vats stand along the wall under an empty washing line.

There is no timer and no lives. You tap a vat, tap another, and the dye pours. When a vat holds one colour a cork goes in. When every vat is corked the level is done, and the game tells you how many pours you used and what the fewest possible was.

The yard teaches as it goes. A small jar holds less. A tinted jar takes only its own dye. Murky dye hides what lies beneath until you pour off the top. A narrow neck pours one layer at a time.

World

The yard stands at the edge of a town at dusk. Roofs lie dark against the last light, a brick wall runs along the lane, and a washing line hangs between two posts. Everything, the sky, the roofs, the glass, the dye and the cloths, is drawn in the game itself.

The five sets

First Pours
Levels 1 to 20. Two colours, then three and four, and a first small jar.
Small Jars
Levels 21 to 40. Five colours, jars that hold less, and the first tinted glass.
Tinted Glass
Levels 41 to 60. Glass that takes one dye only, a sixth colour, and a drip neck.
Murky Dye
Levels 61 to 80. Murk hides what lies beneath, and the vats grow taller.
The Long Vats
Levels 81 to 100. Five layers to a vat, and a seventh colour to finish.
