Overview

Epoch is a two-lane tower battle played with cards. You spend energy to put fighters on the field, push a lane, and break the other side's gate before three minutes run out. If the towers are level, the next one to fall decides it.

Pick one of six peoples and play against the computer, on a phone or a desktop.

Each people has eight cards: six roles (shield, blade, swarm, bow, horse and siege), a hero and a spell. The roles answer one another. A shield stops blades and horses, a swarm tears down a shield, a horse runs down bows and siege engines, and a hit from the right role lands for 60 percent more. A hand is a question about what the other side has just played.

The rules and the cards are plain and public. The names, pictures, sounds and words are written for this game.

The making

Where the idea came from

Epoch began on 1 November 2026, when a card battle between two armies of one war was set aside for a wider question: what if each side came from a different age and country, with its own weapons and its own way of fighting? Six peoples were chosen that day: Rome, the Vikings, the Marathas, the Aztecs, the Samurai and Egypt.

The match was set at two lanes, two bridges over a moat, two towers a side and a three-minute clock with sudden death. One rule followed a week later and has held since: no people may be another in different colours. Each has its own ground, its own shapes, its own banner, hero and spell. The heroes are invented figures, never gods and never real people.

Built with
Plain JavaScript drawn on an HTML canvas, with every sound made in code through Web Audio. It ships as one file. A second build draws the same match in real 3D. No game engine.

Story

Six peoples meet across one river, and each fights in its own way.

Rome drills its shields shoulder to shoulder. The Vikings are slow to leave the shore and hard to stop once they are over the water. The Marathas fight from the fort, cheap and quick when they stay close to home. The Aztecs pay for the next card with every kill. The Samurai let a few good fighters find the opening. Egypt outlasts everyone behind walls that were old before anyone else arrived.

Each people has a hero with one move of its own, from Rome's Testudo to Egypt's Sandstorm, and a spell that turns a lane, from a volley of javelins to a rising flood.

The six peoples

  • Rome

    The Legions

    Drilled ranks and iron patience. Keep your shields shoulder to shoulder. Tortoise: Shields standing next to each other take 30% less damage. Hero: The Centurion, with the move Testudo. Spell: Pilum volley.

  • Vikings

    The Raiders

    Slow to leave the shore, hard to stop once over the water. Sea legs: Units move 40% faster once they have crossed the moat. Hero: The Jarl, with the move Berserk. Spell: Raven's call.

  • Marathas

    The Hill Forts

    Fight from the fort. Cheap and quick when you stay close to home. Hill craft: Cards cost 1 less when played in the back third of your own half. Hero: The Raje, with the move Night raid. Spell: Ghorpad.

  • Aztecs

    The Causeway Kings

    Every kill pays for the next card, so keep fighting. Blood price: Every enemy unit felled gives back 1 energy. Hero: The Eagle Knight, with the move Sky fall. Spell: Flower war.

  • Samurai

    The Castle Lords

    A few good fighters beat a crowd. Let them find the opening. One cut: Single units deal a double-damage critical hit on every fourth strike. Hero: The Ronin, with the move Duel. Spell: Kiai.

  • Egypt

    The Temple Walls

    Outlast them. The walls were old before anyone else arrived. Eternal stone: Towers have 20% more health and mend 1% a second when no enemy is near. Hero: The Pharaoh's Champion, with the move Sandstorm. Spell: Flood.
