Overview

Nobodyssey is the Odyssey told as a game: pixel-art people in lit 3D places, fights you play in real time, and talks where you choose between words, a gift, a craft or waiting.

Book One is free to play on a phone, a tablet or a computer. It has no ads, nothing to buy and no chat. Saves stay on your own device. It is rated Teen, 13 and up: the fights hit hard and men can die, but there is no blood or gore.

The making

Where the idea came from

Nobodyssey began on 8 October 2026 as Homer's Odyssey told as a game of a ship and its crew: twelve ships, six hundred men, and an opening scene in which the hero's name is spelled out letter by letter. For a day it was called Twelve Ships, then Wine-Dark Sea, and an opening chapter at Troy with the wooden horse was added.

The name came the next day, from the trick in the Cyclops' cave where Odysseus says his name is Nobody. Book One ends on that trick. Homer is the only source: the Greek text is kept beside two old translations, and the story is checked against them.

On 1 November 2026 the game was rebuilt as pixel people standing in lit 3D places, keeping its script, its pixel sheets and its music.

Built with
Plain JavaScript. Pixel-art people stand in lit 3D places drawn with WebGL through the three.js library, and the music is played live by the game's own synthesiser through Web Audio. It ships as a web page that keeps working with no signal. No game engine.
Chapters
Book One is told in four chapters across nine places, from the hall of Alcinous to Aeolia.

Story

Troy has fallen. Twelve ships and six hundred men turn for home, and you are the one who has to bring them there. The story follows Homer's Odyssey.

Book One opens in the hall of King Alcinous, where a stranger at the feast is asked who he is. He begins to tell it. A beach at Ismaros and a feast that goes on too long. A shore where nobody wants to leave. An island full of goats. Then a cave, a giant, and a name that saves your life.

Every chapter is one island, one trick, one price. The book closes with the gift of the bag of winds.

World

The book follows Odysseus from the beach at Troy to the cave of the Cyclops, island by island: the shore at Ismaros, where the feast goes on too long; the Lotus Shore, where the fruit makes men forget; and the island of goats, with the cave on its cliff. A sea-chart shows the way between them, and a storm off Cape Malea pushes the fleet off course.

Each chapter opens and closes in the hall of King Alcinous on Scheria, where Odysseus tells the story by the fire and the bard Demodocus listens. It is a told tale, and the hall comes back between the islands.

Press a map to open it. Drag to move around it; scroll, pinch or use the buttons to zoom.

Cast

  • Odysseus

    The player, king of Ithaca

    Clever, proud and loyal to his men. His pride is what brings the curse. "Nobody packed. I finished my cup."

  • Eurylochus

    Second in command

    The sceptic. He argues with Odysseus and is often right. "Every fire's out. Go to sleep, captain. I'll count the snoring."

  • Polites

    Odysseus' closest friend among the crew

    Warm and sensible. He teases his captain. "Your guest-gift, captain. You waited long enough."

  • Elpenor

    The youngest of the crew

    Eager and clumsy, and he falls asleep in warm places. "I climb. Masts, roofs, anything. I've only fallen off twice."

  • Perimedes

    The quiet veteran

    Ropes and signals, a steady hand. "Light first. Then blade."

  • Antiphus

    A man of the crew

    A gambler who keeps his father's sums. He throws the lots in the cave.

  • Damon

    A man of the crew

    He says the crew's names so the sea does not get them. On the Lotus Shore he asks to be left. "Put me down anyway."

  • Maron

    Priest of Apollo at Ismaros

    Odysseus protects his household, and Maron gives him the dark wine that sends the Cyclops to sleep.

  • Alcinous

    King of Scheria

    The host of the hall where the story is told. "Well. We have plenty."

  • Arete

    Queen of Scheria

    She listens, and she asks the hard question.

  • Demodocus

    The bard in the hall

    Blind, and he sings what happened. "I only sing what happened."

  • Polyphemus

    The Cyclops, son of Poseidon

    A huge shepherd who loves his flock, above all his lead ram. Frightening, but not evil for its own sake.

Four chapters

The Oath: Troy
Dawn on the beach below Troy. Odysseus swears to bring every ship and every man home. The player learns to walk, to talk and to read the Oath counter at the top of the screen.
I. Ismaros
The crews take a town and then stay to feast. The Cicones come back from the hills. The player rallies the men against the clock, from afternoon through the night to dawn, and protects the house of Maron, priest of Apollo, who gives a gift of dark wine.
II. The Lotus Shore
Men eat the lotus and forget home. The player finds three scouts and brings each back with a reminder of what he left.
III. Nobody
The cave of the Cyclops. The fight is a trial of wits: the Cyclops shows a tell, and the player answers with Words, Gift, Craft or Wait. It ends under the rams, with a name told and a curse spoken.
