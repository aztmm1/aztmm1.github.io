Overview

Rangmala opens on a shelf with three books, Big Shapes, Patterns and Fine Work, each a tab of its own, and the pages in each grouped by festival. Each card shows the page as the child has left it, with a ring for how much is coloured and a marigold when it is done.

On a page, the child picks one of twelve pots of powder and taps a shape. The colour spreads from the touch. Undo takes back the last shape, and nothing is ever lost by a stray tap.

On the finer pages a child can pinch, or press the plus button, to look closer, up to four times; a small map in the corner shows where on the page they are. A tap on a line, or just beside an edge, fills the nearest shape, so small fingers are not punished for missing by a hair.

When the last shape is filled the page comes alive: flames flicker, the sea swells, a tail shimmers, and petals fall.

The rangoli maker is a round slate floor. Every stroke of powder repeats around the centre, mirrored, so each mark makes a whole flower with six, eight or twelve petals. Finished rangolis rest on the shelf.

A small corner for grown-ups holds the sound switch and a way to clear the book. There is nothing to buy, nothing to sign in to, and no way out of the book to anywhere else.

The making

Where the idea came from

Rangmala began on 11 November 2026 with a wish for a colouring game for children, drawn from Indian festivals and stories. It started as a single page, a courtyard at Diwali, and was chosen to grow into a book.

Two rules shaped it. The stories are told without drawing gods or people, so animals, lamps, sweets and flowers carry them. And every shape must be big enough for a small finger, which is why the pages are bold where other colouring books are fine.

Built with
Plain JavaScript. The pages are drawn as shapes, not pictures, so they stay sharp at any size. It ships as one file that opens in any modern browser, and it sends nothing anywhere. No engine and no libraries.

Story

A book of festivals for small hands. Each page is one moment of a festival or a story, drawn in bold shapes with room for a fingertip.

The stories are told through animals, lamps, sweets and flowers. No gods and no people are drawn.

Nothing needs reading. A back arrow, an undo, twelve pots of powder and a ring that fills as the page does.
