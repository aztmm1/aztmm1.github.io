Overview

Waylight has one hundred levels in five sets of twenty. Press a lantern and drag to its twin to draw a path. Paths cannot share a stone: dragging across another path cuts it back. A court is solved when every pair is joined and every stone is lit, and every court has exactly one answer.

The first levels teach by showing. A drawn hand draws the first path, and a page of four small pictures, How to play, is always one tap away.

New things arrive every few levels: a wider court, urns, tinted stones, longer courts, bridges, and more pairs to join. The climb is steep: the last set is played on courts of more than sixty stones. Every level has three boards, and the game deals a gentler one after a hard patch.

Every fifth level carries a wish: never cut a path, use one stroke for each pair, or join one pair last. Keep the wish and a sky lantern rises. Each solve earns up to three flames, there are twenty awards to earn, and a daily light.

Undo, restart and a hint are always in reach. Leave a board half done and it waits for you: the next time you open the game, Continue puts you back on it, move for move.

The making

Where the idea came from

Waylight began on 11 November 2026 as one of a handful of one-level samples, made alongside Dyeworks to find the next small game after Setstones. It was liked at once and chosen to go on.

Path puzzles are usually coloured dots on a black grid. This one is a paved court at dusk, with lanterns for dots and light for lines. One rule was fixed early: every court has exactly one answer, so a solved court is never luck.

Built with
Plain JavaScript, drawn on an HTML canvas, with sound through Web Audio. It ships as one file that opens in any modern browser. No engine and no libraries.
Levels
One hundred levels in five sets, climbing from a court of sixteen stones with three pairs of lanterns to one of eighty stones with nine. Every level has three boards, three hundred in all, each with exactly one answer. Twenty levels carry a wish, and there is a daily light.

Story

A paved court at the end of the day. The sun has gone behind the hills, and lanterns stand in pairs on the stones, waiting to be joined.

There is no timer and no lives. You press a lantern, draw a path to its twin, and the stones under your finger take the light. When every pair is joined and every stone is lit the court is done, and the game tells you how many strokes you used.

The court teaches as it goes. No path may cross an urn. A tinted stone takes only its own colour's light. Two paths may cross at a bridge, one over, one under.

World

The court stands above still water at dusk, on the night of a lantern festival. Sky lanterns rise over hills in mist, small lamps float below, and the paving is old carved stone with moss in its joints. Everything, the sky, the water, the paving, the paper lanterns and their light, the urns and the bridges, is drawn in the game itself.

The five sets

First Lights
Levels 1 to 20. Small courts, then wider ones, and the first urn.
The Pillared Court
Levels 21 to 40. Urns in the way, six pairs and seven stones across, tinted stones and a taller court.
Tinted Stones
Levels 41 to 60. Light that keeps to its own colour, and the first bridge.
The Bridges
Levels 61 to 80. Paths that cross, over and under, on a court eight stones wide.
The Long Night
Levels 81 to 100. The longest courts, and eight or nine pairs to join.
